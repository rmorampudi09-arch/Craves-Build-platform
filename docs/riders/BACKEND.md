# Rider backend

The Craves rider fleet lives in integration-service, package `in.craves.integration.fleet`, with its own schema, `fleet_schema`, in the integration database. Four other services carry small parts of it: auth-service (the RIDER role), notification-service (offer pushes and ops alerts), user-chef-service (road distance) and order-service (the customer's delivery code). Plan: project doc `rider-app/PLAN.md`. Contracts: `contracts/openapi/rider-v1.yaml` (rider app, with every error code per route) and `contracts/openapi/admin-fleet-v1.yaml` (ops console).

Nothing here changes what production does until a person turns it on: every flag is off by default, `dispatch_policy` is seeded in mode `off`, and test mode is on. The go-live runbook, from merge to a live pilot zone with every outside account to set up, is `GO-LIVE.md`.

## Deploy order

1. **order-service**: V41 (delivery code), the internal verify endpoint, the customer's `deliveryCode`, the order number in Borzo and Pidge notes, and acceptance of a Craves rider's order handed to Borzo, Pidge or (before pickup) another Craves rider.
2. **notification-service**: V8 (the device's `app`), `/internal/v1/push/realtime` and `/internal/v1/ops-alerts`.
3. **user-chef-service**: V20 (road-distance cache) and `/internal/v1/maps/road-distance`.
4. **auth-service**: V20 (the RIDER role) and `/internal/v1/roles/rider/grant` and `/revoke`.
5. **integration-service**: V152 to V177 and the fleet package, all flags off.
6. **APIM** (`scripts/apim/configure-rider-fleet-apim.sh`), once integration-service answers: the gateway probes every route.

The first four only add code that nothing calls until integration-service does. Each service's controllers are in its `live-runtime-lock.txt`.

| `CRAVES_FLEET_RAIN_AUTO` | Rain mode's automatic switch (`DISPATCH.md`, "Rain mode"; `FleetRain`): while the console's switch is off, rain mode comes on by itself when at least 10 rides to the door ended in the last 30 minutes and the middle one took 1.3 times the travel-time model's minutes; it reads nothing unless `CRAVES_FLEET_ETA_MODE` is `live`. Operational values live in versioned tables, not variables, so changing them needs no restart: `dispatch_policy` (seeded `2026-10-v1`, mode `off`, offers 40 s, fleet stage 60 s, radius 5 km as a placeholder; V154 and V155 add pickup detour 130%, per-order overhead ₹5, cost-guard premium ₹10, Pidge stage 600 s) and `rate_card` (seeded draft `2026-10-v1`: ₹40 base for the first 2 km of the drop, ₹9 a drop km after that, ₹9 a pickup km after the first 1 km, ₹1.50 a minute of waiting after 8 free minutes, ₹80 an hour guaranteed in a booked slot; V154 adds ₹20 cancellation at the kitchen, ₹9 a km to return food and a 10-minute unreachable wait). All are draft business inputs with declared ranges. Ops publishes both from the console (`/rate-cards`, `/dispatch-policies`); `FleetPolicyStore` holds the rules and the V159 triggers hold the same notice rules, so SQL cannot skip them. Telangana Act 21 of 2026, s.13(2) asks for 14 days' notice of changed terms:

- **Rate card** (the riders' pay terms): the first one ever published may start at once (one whose start has passed starts when published); every later one must start at least 14 days after publishing.
- **Dispatch policy** (operations): a version starts now, or on an `effectiveFrom` at least 14 days away (a nearer one is 422 `NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`). The pickup detour is part of pay (pickup km = straight line × detour), and the terms show it ("Pickup distance: straight line × 1.3"). So once a rate card is published, a detour other than the one riders are shown (the policy in force, not withdrawn; else the seeded draft's) needs a start at least 14 days away (422 `DETOUR_NEEDS_NOTICE`). The same goes for how orders are offered (Phase 4, V170: `offers_at_once` 1 to 3, `offer_rounds` 1 to 5, `fair_band_m` 0 to 5000; defaults 3, 1, 0, the rules before Phase 4): riders read them under Terms, so a change to any of them needs that notice (422 `RULES_NEED_NOTICE`). They are checked against those fixed bounds, not declared ranges, and with more than one round, rounds × offer seconds must fit in the fleet stage. Before any card is published, riders have no terms and nothing needs notice, which is when `GO-LIVE.md` §6 sets them. `live` needs a published rate card in force (422 `RATE_CARD_REQUIRED`); behind that, `FleetDispatcher` opens no fleet stage in `live` without one (it logs `FLEET_SKIPPED reason=NO_RATE_CARD_IN_FORCE` at most once per 5 s).
- **Emergency stop**: an `off` without `effectiveFrom` always works. It keeps the values riders are shown (the 7 values the page sent are ignored, so a stale page can still stop), starts at once and withdraws every version scheduled for later (`withdrawn_at`, `withdrawn_by`; the audit names them), so dispatch stays stopped. A withdrawn version never counts as in force and cannot be un-withdrawn. Any other version set now leaves a scheduled one to take over on its date (the console warns).
- **Upcoming**: while a rate card is in force, riders see the next change as `upcoming` in their terms during its notice: the earliest of the next published card, the next detour change and the next change to the offer rules, with the lines and the rules they will have then (`upcoming.offerRules`).
- **Ranges**: values must lie inside the declared ranges of the newest published version (a first rate card declares its own; before any policy is published, the seeded draft's). A card published after the first takes the published ranges, replacing the ones its draft declared (the audit says so).
- **Published versions never change or go away.** The V159 triggers refuse deleting a published rate card or dispatch policy, any change to a published rate card, and any change to a published dispatch policy except one withdrawal before it starts; they set `published_at` and `withdrawn_at` from the database clock, so a caller cannot backdate them. The code reads the same clock (`SELECT now()` in the transaction), which is also what every in-force read uses; in-force reads break ties on `effective_from` by the newest `created_at`, in the console too.
- **One version per moment**: a version that starts later may not start at the same moment as another published one in its table (a withdrawn policy does not count), or which one is in force, or next, would be a guess: 409 `START_TAKEN` ("Another version starts at that moment. Pick another start time."), in the code and the trigger. Versions that start now never collide: each publish has its own transaction and clock.
- Publishes take an advisory lock per table (in the code and in the trigger), so two "first" rate cards cannot both start at once.

Agents never change the mode.

#### Idempotency keys

A repeated key is the same request and gets its first result. Since Phase C (V177) a trip's key is bound to the request it first came with: `FleetTripService.requestHash` is the SHA-256 hex of `POST /api/v1/rider{path}`, a newline, and the fields below as canonical JSON (keys sorted, absent fields left out). It is stored as `trip_event.request_hash` beside the key. The same key with another request is 422 `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` ("This step was already sent with different details. Open the trip again.") and changes nothing. A row from before V177 has no hash and replays as before.

| Route | Hashed with the method and path | Left out, and why |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /trips/{id}/arrived-pickup`, `food-not-ready`, `picked-up`, `arrived-drop` | nothing | The body is only the tap (`ageMs`, `fix`). Every retry sends it again, aged, and a second "food not ready" tap keeps the step's key with a new tap. |
| `POST /trips/{id}/failed` | `reason`, `note` (a blank note counts as none) | `ageMs`, `fix`. |
| `POST /trips/{id}/delivered` (JSON) | `method` (`OTP`), `otp` | `ageMs`, `fix`, and `capturedOffline`, which the app adds once the tap is deferred. The app makes a new key for every code typed (`-{attempt}`). The hash holds the code but is stored only with a refused code or with the delivery that spent it. |
| `POST /trips/{id}/delivered` (multipart) | `method` (`PHOTO`), `callsMade`, `photo` (the SHA-256 of its bytes) | `ageMs`, `fix`. Every retry sends the photo kept at the tap (`keepPhoto`), byte for byte. A retry without its photo is compared with nothing: all it can get back is the delivered trip (P1-07). |
| `POST /offers/{id}/accept` | nothing (no body) | The `ACCEPTED` event keeps its key and hash, so the accept's key can't replay as a trip step. A repeated accept is decided by the offer's state. |

`decline` stores nothing. `POST /withdrawals` compares its one field, `amountPaise`, itself (409 `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`). A delivery checks its key again in the transaction that stores it, so a request with the same key that landed while order-service checked the code, or during the photo upload, gives that request's answer or 422. The generic Idempotency-Key filter (CH-07, `payment_schema.idempotency_record`) skips `/api/v1/rider/trips/**` (`craves.idempotency.excluded-paths`): it fingerprints the raw body, `ageMs` included, so the retry of a step sent with the app's token that had gone through, but whose answer was lost, got its 422 and the app dropped the trip's queued steps. Offers and withdrawals still go through it.

| user-chef-service | `POST /internal/v1/maps/road-distance` | `{kitchenId?, addressId?, from, to}` → `{meters, seconds, source: OLA, CACHE or FALLBACK}`; Ola Directions with a 3 s deadline, cached 30 days; on any failure straight line × 1.3, not cached. |

Never in the chef's order view, logs or events. One read-only transaction (one pool connection) per call. |

## Riders, phones and shifts

### Due process (two tracks)

Nothing blocks a rider automatically, and declining or missing offers never changes anything.

- `SAFETY_PAUSE`: the rider is SUSPENDED now; `reviewBy` must be within 72 hours.
- `NOTICE`: changes nothing for 7 days; `deadline` at least 7 days ahead; the rider keeps working and sees the notice.
- `RESTORE`: back to ACTIVE. From PENDING it activates the rider (who must have signed in once) and grants RIDER in auth-service.
- `DEACTIVATE`: only after a safety pause, or once an open notice's deadline has passed; revokes RIDER.

Every event records the reason, the evidence summary, the approver and the appeal phone (required except for RESTORE). The table's CHECKs hold the 7-day and 72-hour limits too.

### Payout accounts

The full number (or UPI ID) is stored only as `BankDataCipher` stores chef bank details: AES-256-GCM with the versioned bank-data keys. Uniqueness uses a keyed HMAC of `UPI:<id>` or `BANK:<first 4 of IFSC>:<number>`, so the same account at another branch of the same bank still matches. Rotating the active bank key changes new fingerprints, so re-fingerprint current accounts when rotating. The holder name matches the KYC name when both have the same words, in any order, after removing accents, case, punctuation and the titles Mr, Mrs, Ms, Miss, Shri, Smt, Kumari and Dr; initials are words, so "R Kumar" does not match "Ravi Kumar".

### Phones

One bound phone per rider. `installationId` is the rider app's Android `ANDROID_ID`; the `rider_device` id is a name-based UUID of the rider id and the installation id, so the installation id itself is never stored.

- The rider's first installation is BOUND. The same installation again updates `fcm_token`, `app_version`, `model` and `last_seen_at`.
- Any other installation is stored PENDING_APPROVAL and answered 409 `DEVICE_NOT_BOUND`. A REPLACED phone that registers again goes back to PENDING_APPROVAL.
- Ops approves it (`POST .../devices/{deviceId}/approve`): it becomes the one BOUND phone, the old one REPLACED, and an open shift's credential is revoked (the old phone gets a stop on its next `/locations`).
- With the shift credential instead of a JWT, the rider is the credential's (it must be live, else 401 `SHIFT_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`).

Offer pushes go to the BOUND phone's `fcm_token`.

### Shifts and the shift credential

`POST /shift/start` checks, in this order:

| Status | Code | When |
|---|---|---|
| 403 | `RIDER_NOT_ACTIVE` | The rider is not ACTIVE. |
| 409 | `DEVICE_NOT_BOUND` | `X-Craves-Installation-Id` is missing or is not the rider's bound phone. |
| 403 | `APP_UPDATE_REQUIRED` | The bound phone's `appVersion` is below `minAppVersion` (403, not 426: 426 means an HTTP protocol upgrade). Checked only here, so a running shift is never cut off. |
| 403 | `DOCUMENTS_EXPIRED` | The DL, RC, insurance or PUC has no VERIFIED copy whose `expires_on` is today (IST) or later; a missing expiry counts as expired. The message names them. |
| 422 | `SELFIE_INVALID` | Not a JPEG, empty, or over 5 MB. |
| 409 | `SHIFT_OPEN` | A shift is open on another phone. |

The selfie goes to `riders/<riderId>/selfies/<shiftId>.jpg`, PENDING in the console's selfie queue. The credential is 32 random bytes, base64url; only its SHA-256 (hex) is stored in `rider_shift.credential_hash`. It expires 12 hours after the shift started (the table's CHECK) and is valid only for `/locations` and `/devices`: Spring Security lets only those two routes through without a JWT, and `FleetShiftCredentialSecurityTest` walks every rider and admin route with only a credential to prove that only these two answer. With `CRAVES_FLEET_TRIP_SHIFT_CREDENTIAL_ENABLED` (P2-01, off) it also sends trip steps, for the phone's native outbox: `FleetShiftService.tripIdentity` turns it into its rider, refuses it once the shift ended or the rider was deactivated (401 `SHIFT_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`), and lets it move only that rider's trip in progress or one that ended during this shift, so a retried step still gets its first answer (any other trip: 404 `TRIP_NOT_FOUND`). A suspended rider can still finish the trip in hand, as with the app's token. `FleetTripCredentialSecurityTest` walks the routes again with the setting on: only those two and the trip steps answer. A second start from the same phone while its shift is open (the app lost the credential) rotates that shift's credential and keeps the first selfie. A shift past its 12 hours, or whose credential ops revoked, is closed at the next start.

### Location ingest

`POST /api/v1/rider/locations` body `{fixes: [{lat, lng, accuracyM, at, mock, ageMs?}], offerReceipts?}`, 0 to 20 fixes (more is 400; an empty list is the heartbeat). A phone back from a dead zone sends the fixes it kept, oldest first (P2-03); apps before it send at most 5.

- **Who.** With `X-Craves-Shift-Credential` the shift is the one whose `credential_hash` matches (unknown: 401). Without it, a RIDER JWT and the rider's open shift (none: a stop).
- **Fixes.** Fixes with impossible values, from before the shift started or more than 5 minutes ahead of the database clock are dropped. Only fixes newer than the shift's stored latest fix are used, so a resent batch writes nothing twice. A fix with `ageMs` (app 1.1.0) is timed by the database clock, `now() - ageMs` (at most an hour), before these checks, so a wrong phone clock no longer matters. Without `ageMs`, `at` (the phone's fix time) is used as before, and a phone whose clock runs more than 5 minutes fast sends no usable fixes (its heartbeat, offers and stop still work).
- **Writes.** `rider_position_latest` (the newest fix wins, with `mock`, `shift_id` and the trip in progress); `trip_track` (every new fix, with `mock`, only while a trip is in progress, ASSIGNED to AT_DROP); in-zone seconds (below).
- **Offer receipts.** Optional `offerReceipts: [{offerId, channel, ageMs}]` (app 1.1.0, first 10 used): for each of the rider's own offers with no `rang_at` yet, `rang_at = now() - ageMs` (never before `offered_at`) and `rang_channel` (`fcm` or `locations`), in the shift-row statement. A bad id or channel is dropped, never a refused call. With `pushed_at` and `push_result` (stored by `FleetDispatcher` after each push: `SENT`, `NO_TOKEN`, notification-service's code or `ERROR`) they give offer-to-ring per phone (`PILOT-HEALTH.md`).
- **Phone health.** Optional `health` (app 1.1.0, about once a minute): merged into `rider_shift.health` key by key (each keeps its latest value; a call without it changes nothing) and `can_ring` stored, in the shift-row statement. A `lastExitReason` is logged as `FLEET_APP_EXIT rider=… reason=…` (one line, at most 200 characters).
- **Heartbeat.** Every call that isn't a stop, with or without fixes, updates the shift row: `last_seen_at = now()`, `max_silence_seconds` (the longest gap between two calls; the first gap counts from `started_at`) and `silences_over_60s`. Heading home (Phase 5) ends in the same statement when the newest real (not mock) fix of the call is within 1 km of the rider's home spot. Dispatch and ops reassign read `last_seen_at`, not `rider_position_latest.received_at`, which only moves when a newer fix arrives. The watchdog counts either, so it never takes a trip from a rider whose positions still arrive (also while a deploy's old revision, which doesn't write `last_seen_at`, still serves `/locations`).
- **Answer.** `{offers, activeTrip?, stop?, stopReason?, maxFixes?, nextPollSec?}`. `maxFixes` (20) tells the app how many fixes the next call may carry, so an app never sends more than its server takes (an app that sees none sends 5). `nextPollSec` is how often a phone waiting for offers uploads: `CRAVES_FLEET_IDLE_POLL_SECONDS`, 10 by default, kept within 5 to 30 (P2-04); lower it only if offer receipts show pushes ringing late on a phone brand (BLUEPRINT C1, `PILOT-HEALTH.md`). `callbackUploads: true` while `CRAVES_FLEET_CALLBACK_UPLOADS_ENABLED` is on (P2-10). A stop carries none of the three. `offers`: the rider's ACTIVE offers not yet expired, built by `FleetTripModels.offer`, the one mapping the offer push uses too: `expiresInSec` by the database clock, `kitchenArea`, `customerLocality`, `itemsCount` (left out while 0), `payLines` from `trip_offer.pay_lines` (`[{component, label, amountPaise}]`), `estMinutes` = (pickup m + drop m) / 333 rounded up, + 5 (with the travel-time model live, its two legs' minutes rounded up, + 5), and `reason`, why this rider got it (`trip_offer.reason`; absent on offers made before V170). `activeTrip`: `{tripId, step, version}` with a rider-v1 TripStep (`FleetTripModels.step`); an ended or reassigned trip is reported with its final step on every call while it changed in the last 2 minutes or after the phone's previous position (on the same shift), so at least two calls report it, also after a dead zone; the app leaves trip mode and says "Trip cancelled" even if one response is lost.
- **Stop.** `{offers: [], stop: true, stopReason}` and the shift is closed when the rider is not ACTIVE, the shift was ended, its credential was revoked, or its 12 hours are up.

**Zone model.** There is no zone geometry yet: `rider_slot` holds a zone name and a time window, `dispatch_policy` only a radius. So the gap between two consecutive non-mock fixes counts while one of the rider's BOOKED slots is running, clipped to that slot and at most 60 seconds per gap. It is added to that slot's `in_zone_seconds` (what the guarantee settles on) and to the shift's, in one statement; a slot that settled meanwhile keeps its settled seconds. Upgrade: add zone geometry when ops draws the pilot area and test each fix against it.

**Statement budget.** One call runs at most 5 statements in one transaction: (1) read the shift, rider, latest position, trip in progress, previous trip and running slot, locking the shift row; (2) upsert `rider_position_latest`; (3) one multi-row `trip_track` insert, only on a trip; (4) update the shift row on every call: the heartbeat, the survival counters and the in-zone seconds of the shift and its slot, in one statement; (5) read the offers. A stop runs statement 1 and one shift update. `FleetLocationApiDatabaseTest.twoHundredCallsStayWithinFiveStatementsEach` counts them. With the fleet engine on (Phase 5), statement 1 also reads the credential's expiry, the rider's choices and home spot, the break and the open offer (one more join, on the ACTIVE-offer index) and statement 4 returns `can_ring` and `heading_home_until`, for the engine's PING: still 5 statements. Off, both are unchanged. The load target, 20 calls a second for 10 minutes, is at most 60,000 statements (100 a second) on one pooled connection per call for a few milliseconds. EXPLAIN on Postgres 16 with 2,000 riders, 62,000 shifts, 200,500 trips and offers and 42,000 slots uses an index for every table (the credential is cast to `CHAR(64)` so its unique index applies), and the track insert goes straight to the day's partition.

## Dispatch and trips

### Dispatch chain

`dispatch_policy` mode governs it; with no published policy in force, or mode `off`, nothing below happens and Borzo and Pidge run exactly as before.

1. The delivery command (`DeliveryCommandWorker`) asks `DeliveryProviderRouter.fleetStage` first. `FleetDispatcher` plans: the chef from the checkout's finance quote (`payment_schema.finance_issued_snapshot`; unknown chef: no fleet), test mode, the drop's road distance, eligible riders (ACTIVE, open shift and not on a break, no active trip or open offer, with rider choices on within the rider's farthest pickup and drop and, for a rider heading home, only orders whose drop leaves them at least 500 m nearer home and adds at most 2 km (or a quarter of their way home) to riding straight home (`FleetDispatcher.homeNearerM`, straight lines; Phase 5), heard from: a `/locations` call in the last 45 s by `rider_shift.last_seen_at`, a real fix under 3 minutes old, and a phone that can ring (`can_ring` not false), inside the policy radius; a fix's age comes from its `ageMs` on app 1.1.0, but app 1.0 sends none, so its fix time is the phone's own clock and a 1.0 phone running more than 3 minutes slow gets no offers: pin `minAppVersion` 1.1.0 before dispatch goes live), pay per rider and the cost guard (locked pay + overhead ≤ the next courier's live quote + premium; no quote: riders may go). `shadow` logs `FLEET_SHADOW` and stops there.
2. `live`: one transaction creates the trip (`SEARCHING`, order code `#` + the first 8 characters of the sub-order id, as the chef and customer see it), the fleet stage (deadline = fleet stage seconds) and the first round of offers, as the policy's rules say (Phase 4, `DISPATCH.md` §2): `offers_at_once` riders (3 before Phase 4), nearest first (by straight line; by road among the 5 nearest with `CRAVES_FLEET_ROAD_RANKING_ENABLED`; or, with the travel-time model live, by its minutes to the kitchen among the 50 nearest: "The travel-time model" below) or, with a fair band, fair turns within it. Pay, rate card version and both distances are locked; each offer keeps its `round` and `reason`. Each offer is pushed with TTL = seconds left. Borzo's 120-second window has not started.
3. With `offer_rounds` above 1, while the stage lasts and no offer is open, the next round goes to the next riders not yet asked about this order, ranked again (`FleetDispatcher.nextRound`; the stage row is locked, so two workers never send the same round). The delivery command looks again every 5 s while rounds run (else 10 s), so the next rider hears within seconds of a decline.
   - A round goes out only if the stage still has a full `offer_seconds` left. If not, the stage closes and Borzo is next, so no rider's time to decide is cut short.
   - If round 1's riders became busy just before their offers were written, the stage stays open, and the next look sends round 1 to the riders free then.
   - Closing before the deadline takes the stage row's lock first and checks again for an open offer (`closeUnlessDeciding`), so a round another worker sent meanwhile is never withdrawn.
4. The fleet stage closes on an accept (in the accept's own transaction: delivery job `craves_fleet`, `COURIER_ASSIGNED` with the rider card) or, at its deadline or once no offer is open and no round is left, by a conditional update on the same stage row; then `BORZO` opens for 120 s (when the timed policy is ready, else `PIDGE`) and routing books as before. A late accept finds the stage closed: 410.
5. The dispatch tick (`FleetDispatchJobs`, every 5 s) expires offers, moves `BORZO` to `PIDGE` at its deadline and closes a stage when its courier is assigned. A Pidge stage past its deadline, a courier who cancelled (not one Craves cancelled, step 7), or an uncertain create (never retried) goes to `OPS_QUEUE` with the ops alert `FLEET_DISPATCH_QUEUE`; a courier booked after all clears the queue entry.
6. Watchdog, on the heartbeat (`rider_shift.last_seen_at`, set by every location call with or without a new fix, or a position received): a rider on the way to the kitchen (`ASSIGNED`) whose phone has not called for 90 s loses the trip. At the kitchen (`AT_PICKUP`) the rider may already hold the food, so 90 s silent sends ops one `FLEET_RIDER_SILENT_AT_KITCHEN` (recorded as the trip event `WATCHDOG_ALERTED`) and the trip goes back only at 300 s silent (blueprint E2). Once the chef has handed the rider the food (`trip.chef_handed_over_at`, from order-service's `ORDER_HANDED_OVER`, P2-05) the trip never goes back: the alert then says the food is with the rider, also for a rider who never tapped "arrived" (`ASSIGNED`), and is sent once. order-service writes that event only while `ORDER_HANDED_OVER` is in its `CRAVES_DOMAIN_EVENT_ENABLED_TYPES`, and integration-service records it from the domain-event subscription it already reads (`DeliveryServiceBusProcessors`; an unknown type there would be read as a chef acceptance, so deploy this integration-service first). The trip row is locked before the silence is judged, so a step that commits while the watchdog waits for the row is judged in its new state. A trip that goes back is `CANCELLED` with pickup-leg pay held for ops, ops gets `FLEET_RIDER_LOST`, and the tick books Borzo or Pidge with `handoffFromProviderId: craves_fleet`. order-service accepts that hand-over only from a rider before pickup, clears the rider card the chef sees, and the customer's code goes with it. A later Borzo to Pidge hand-over counts only couriers of that Borzo delivery.
7. Cancellation (R16 item 11). order-service's `ORDER_CANCELLED` (`OrderCancelledEventFactory`; data `{checkoutId, chefSubOrderId, reason, cancelledAt}`, `contracts/events/order-cancelled-v1.schema.json`) arrives on the domain-event subscription integration-service already reads (`DeliveryServiceBusProcessors`) and is stored once in `delivery_schema.order_cancelled` (V176); an event without its `chefSubOrderId` is dead-lettered as invalid. That row, or a refund order-service requested for the sub-order (`payment_schema.refund`, the only signal before the event), is the cancellation signal (`DeliveryCommandRepository.CANCELLED_ORDER`). Today refunds come only before the chef accepts; drop the refund half before order-service ships item-level refunds, or a partial refund would end a delivery. From then on:
   - an accept is 409 `ORDER_CANCELLED`;
   - the delivery command offers no rider and books no courier: it is marked `DEAD_LETTER` with `ORDER_CANCELLED`, and later copies of its message (a ready tap, a decline) complete without a Service Bus dead letter;
   - the next tick ends the trip through `FleetTripService.cancel`: open offers withdrawn, every open stage closed, a rider already on the trip told at once (`TRIP_ENDED`, reason `ORDER_CANCELLED`) and paid for the state reached, not held;
   - a Borzo or Pidge courier already booked for an order order-service cancelled (`ORDER_CANCELLED` only: a refund comes before any courier is booked, and a partial refund must never cancel one) is cancelled by `FleetCourierCancel`, a fleet job of its own every 5 s, so a slow courier never holds up the dispatch tick. One statement stores the intent and claims the attempt in `delivery_schema.courier_cancel` before the courier is called, so two replicas never both call, and the call runs outside any transaction. A confirmed cancel goes through the delivery-status pipeline at once, as their callback would. A refusal (their courier may already have the food) is tried again a minute later, 3 tries in all; then ops gets `FLEET_COURIER_CANCEL_FAILED` (also logged as `CRAVES_ALERT alert=FLEET_COURIER_CANCEL_FAILED`) with the order to cancel it by hand. A Borzo order still inside its 120 s for a courier first stops its switch to Pidge (the handoff goes to `MANUAL_REVIEW`, "Craves cancelled this delivery"); one the handoff worker is switching right now is waited for, and the courier it ends with is the one cancelled. A courier Craves cancelled never sends its stage to the ops queue. Only with `CRAVES_FLEET_ENABLED`: without the fleet the cancel is recorded and nobody new is booked, but a courier already booked must be cancelled by hand.

   order-service writes the event only once it has a cancel after acceptance (none yet) and `ORDER_CANCELLED` is in its `CRAVES_DOMAIN_EVENT_ENABLED_TYPES`. Deploy this integration-service and let the event through the `integration-service-chef-accepted` subscription first (`GO-LIVE.md`): an older integration-service reads it as a chef acceptance and dead-letters it.

8. One writer per rider (1 Nov 2026): an accept and every offer insert first lock the rider row (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`; offers in rider-id order), so an offer for another order waits for a committing accept and then finds the rider busy, instead of ringing on a phone that can no longer take it.
9. A rider whose trip ends without them (ops cancel, release to Borzo or Pidge, hand-over to another rider, or the order cancelled) gets a high-priority `TRIP_ENDED` push once the change commits (`FleetTripService.tellRider`, `RiderPushClient.tripEnded`; data `{type, tripId, reason}` with reason `CANCELLED`, `RELEASED`, `HANDED_TO_RIDER` or `ORDER_CANCELLED`). `/locations` still carries the ended trip.

`craves_fleet` (`CravesFleetAdapter`) is inactive in the catalog: routing never quotes or creates it. `track()` maps the trip to the pipeline; `cancel()` ends a trip as in step 7.

Borzo and Pidge get the same order number: pickup note "Craves order #N. Match this number with the kitchen.", dropoff note "Craves order #N.", matter "Food order #N" (order-service `OrderNumber`). The Craves delivery code is never sent to them.

### The fleet engine (Phase 5)

`services/fleet-engine` (Java 21 and Spring Boot, like the other services) keeps every online rider's position, availability and fair-turn standing in Redis and ranks riders for orders by the same rules as `FleetDispatcher`, in parallel per 0.05° map block (`SCALE.md` has the design, the measured capacity and the switches). Postgres stays the system of record: the engine's state is a cache it can lose and rebuild, `FleetDispatcher` still writes every offer, and the index on ACTIVE offers still allows one per rider. With `CRAVES_FLEET_ENGINE_MODE` off, nothing below runs and Redis is never written.

- **The feed** (`FleetEngineFeed`). Each change that matters to dispatch goes to the engine once its transaction commits, as a protobuf event (`contracts/proto/craves/fleet/v1/fleet.proto`) on the rider's stream `craves:fleet:rq:{rq<p>}` (p: a hash of the rider id, modulo 16):
  - **PING**, every `/locations` call: its fixes and the shift as the call left it (see "Statement budget").
  - **REFRESH**: the rider read again (`FleetEngineFeed.SNAPSHOT`, with the day's standing as `FleetDispatcher.standings` reads it) after offers are made, an accept, a decline, a trip's end with pay (`FleetTripPayFinalized`) and again once its pay is credited, an ops status change, going online, a break and its end, heading home and its end, a home spot or distance choice saved, and for every rider with an open shift every 5 minutes. **OFFLINE** instead when the rider has no working shift.
  - Callers only queue; one thread reads Postgres (one pooled connection at a time) and sends. When `CRAVES_FLEET_ENGINE_QUEUE_CAPACITY` events are waiting, the next is dropped and counted, never waited for, so Redis never slows a rider's call. Each minute's counts are logged as `FLEET_ENGINE_FEED`; failures as `FLEET_ENGINE_FEED_FAILED`, at most once a minute, and a batch lost to one counts in `failed`. The home spot goes out only while heading home uses it.
  - Not refreshed at once (they reach the engine with the rider's next `/locations` call, within the idle poll): an offer withdrawn as its stage closes or lost to another rider's accept, a trip cancelled without pay, and ops moves. Live dispatch checks the engine's riders against Postgres anyway (below).
- **Shadow** (mode `shadow`, or `live` while the dispatch policy is shadow; road ranking off): each time dispatch ranks riders for an order, in every round, it also sends that ranking (up to 50 riders) to the stream of the kitchen's map block, `craves:fleet:dq:{dq<p>}`. The engine ranks the same order as of that moment and logs `FLEET_ENGINE_SHADOW` (`agree=same`, `same_set` or `differ`). Nothing else changes.
- **Live** (mode `live`, the dispatch policy live, road ranking off): dispatch sends the order to the block's stream and waits up to `CRAVES_FLEET_ENGINE_REPLY_TIMEOUT_MS` (300 ms) for the engine's ranking at `craves:fleet:reply:{<request id>}`. The engine holds the riders it names for 5 s, so an order asked at the same moment doesn't get them too; a solve that ends after dispatch stopped waiting holds nobody. Dispatch first keeps only the engine's riders Postgres still lets hear of an order (ACTIVE, on a working shift, no break, no trip, no open offer: one query), logging `FLEET_ENGINE_STALE` when it passes some over, so a seconds-old engine state never costs the order its round. It still applies test mode, the cost guard and every guard of the offer insert (the same conditions, and one ACTIVE offer per rider), then refreshes the riders it offered. Without an answer, or with none still free (`FLEET_ENGINE_FOUND_NOBODY` when dispatch then finds riders), it ranks riders itself exactly as before (`FLEET_ENGINE_FALLBACK`); after 5 failures in a row it stops asking for 30 s (`CRAVES_ALERT alert=FLEET_ENGINE_PAUSED`).
- **Redis.** The service's own Azure Managed Redis connection (`SPRING_DATA_REDIS_URL`), through Lettuce's asynchronous commands on the connection Spring shares: a batch of events costs one round trip and opens no connection. That connection carries auth's checks too, so the engine never queues behind it: nothing is sent while it is down (Lettuce would buffer and replay it all at once), a command not answered in time is cancelled, and after a failure only one caller at a time asks again, after 5 s. Streams are trimmed by age on Redis's own clock (from the entry ids) and expire 3 minutes after their last write. Every key carries a `{...}` hash tag, so each stream lives whole on one shard. The client runs in standalone mode, which reaches every key only on a one-shard cache: check the shard count before shadow (`GO-LIVE.md` §9).

### The travel-time model (Phase 5)

`FleetEta` (`fleet/eta`; the model and its training: `ml/fleet-eta/README.md`; the rules: `DISPATCH.md` §2, "The travel-time model") predicts the minutes of a trip's two legs, rider to kitchen and kitchen to customer, with gradient-boosted trees trained in Python on Craves's own delivered trips and evaluated in Java (`EtaModel`, the same file as the engine's; `scripts/release/verify-platform-copies.py` keeps them identical). Its inputs are places and times only, never who the rider is.

- **Versions** live in `fleet_schema.eta_model` (V174): `version` (the key, and the version the model JSON names: a CHECK), `model` (the trees as JSON, as `python -m fleet_eta sql` writes them), `metrics` (the held-out judgement it was published with: `heldOutTest.verdict` for its minutes, `heldOutTest.rankingVerdict` for its ranking) and `created_at`. Nothing in the services writes the table.
- **The switch.** `CRAVES_FLEET_ETA_MODE`: `off` (the default) loads nothing; `shadow` loads the version in `CRAVES_FLEET_ETA_MODEL_VERSION` and logs, for every order dispatch ranks, the riders today's rule asks first and the ones the model would (`FLEET_ETA_SHADOW chefSubOrderId=… model=… same=… today=… eta=… etaPickupMinutes=…`), changing nothing; `live` shows its minutes and may rank by it (below). An unknown mode logs `CRAVES_ALERT alert=FLEET_ETA_MODE_UNKNOWN` and counts as `off`.
- **Loading.** The version is read once, then kept. A missing setting, a missing row or a model that can't be evaluated (unknown features, a broken tree) logs `CRAVES_ALERT alert=FLEET_ETA_MODEL_UNSET`, `FLEET_ETA_MODEL_MISSING` or `FLEET_ETA_MODEL_REFUSED`, and dispatch keeps today's rule; it is tried again every minute. A good load logs `FLEET_ETA_LOADED version=… mode=… ranking=…`.
- **Live: what is shown.** Each offer stores both legs' minutes (`trip_offer.pickup_minutes`, `drop_minutes`), which its `estMinutes` uses; the accept copies the pickup minutes to the trip for the chef's `pickupEtaAt`. The minutes are kept between 60 km/h on the straight line and 120 minutes. Showing them changes no rule.
- **Live: who hears first.** Only where the dispatch policy in force has `rank_by_travel_time` and the version's `rankingVerdict` is `better` (`FleetEta.ranking`): `FleetDispatcher.byEta` ranks the 50 riders nearest by straight line by predicted minutes to the kitchen, held as metres at 20 km/h so a fair band keeps its unit (each km of band is 3 minutes); any others follow, nearest first, outside the band. It replaces road ranking while it applies, and the offer's reason is `SOONEST`. A policy asking for it with no such version ranks by distance and logs `CRAVES_ALERT alert=FLEET_ETA_NOT_RANKING` (at most every 5 minutes). Otherwise, with a model loaded, the model's order after the same fair turns is logged beside today's (`FLEET_ETA_SHADOW`).
- **The engine.** With the model live and the engine's feed on (`CRAVES_FLEET_ENGINE_MODE` not `off`), integration-service writes the model to Redis at `craves:fleet:model:eta:<version>` (kept 7 days, written again at most every 10 minutes). Once written, and only where riders are ranked by it, it names the version in each request's policy (`eta`, `eta_model`, fields 8 and 9 of `Policy`); until then it ranks those orders itself. The engine loads a version on the first request naming it and keeps the last four; a request naming one it can't load gets no reply (integration-service ranks it) and counts in `etaMissing` (`FLEET_ENGINE_STATS`).

### Ops reassign and cancel

Both go through the dispatch chain, in one transaction, audited and idempotent, so an order never has two couriers recorded. The open stage closes first by the same conditional update an accept uses, and its offers are withdrawn.

- **To a rider** (`riderId`): only an order no rider holds yet (still searching, or in the Borzo, Pidge or ops-queue stage with no courier booked). Outside the fleet stage the delivery command is taken over first, only if it is not booking right now (else 409, try again in a minute). The rider must be ACTIVE, on shift, free, heard from (a call in the last 45 s, a real fix under 3 minutes old and a phone that can ring, as for dispatch) and allowed by test mode. Pay is locked as for an accepted offer, on a published rate card that has started (the trip's own, else the one in force; none: 409 `RATE_CARD_REQUIRED`, "Publish a rate card before giving trips to riders."), then the usual `COURIER_ASSIGNED`. Call the rider first: nothing rings; the trip appears on their next location call.
- **To Borzo or Pidge** (`provider`): a rider's trip before pickup ends for that rider with pay for the trip so far (not held) and that stage opens; the tick books it. An order still searching gets that stage.
- **A rider's trip to another rider** (`riderId`, with `CRAVES_FLEET_RIDER_HANDOVER_ENABLED`, and once the contract migration has dropped `trip_chef_sub_order_id_key`; until then it is refused with "Moving a trip to another rider isn't switched on yet." and logs `FLEET_HANDOVER_WAITS_FOR_MIGRATION`): only before pickup (`ASSIGNED` or `AT_PICKUP`), and only to a rider who could take it now (as above). A trip row is one rider's pay record, so the first rider's trip ends as a release (pay for the trip so far, not held; their app reads "Craves ops gave this order to another rider. You're paid for the trip so far.") and gets `replaced_at`, and the order carries on in a new trip for the second rider that names it in `replaces`, with their own locked pay and an accepted offer. The delivery job follows the new trip, and its `COURIER_ASSIGNED` event names the first trip as the handoff source (`handoffFromProviderId` `craves_fleet`), so order-service shows the chef and the customer the new rider. The answer is the new trip; the replaced one is refused afterwards as `TRIP_CHANGED`. Without the flag this stays refused, as before.
- Refused: anything after pickup (cancel instead), any order Borzo or Pidge already holds, and a rider's trip whose food the chef already handed over (`chef_handed_over_at`, P2-05): another rider or a courier would find an empty kitchen.
- **Cancel**: a rider's trip ends with the trip-state pay rule (not held) and `CANCELLED` reaches the order. An order still searching stops and its delivery command is marked `DEAD_LETTER`. A courier booked at that moment is not recorded and ops gets `FLEET_COURIER_UNRECORDED` to cancel it. An order Borzo or Pidge holds is cancelled with them first, before the transaction and outside it (`FleetCourierCancel.forOps`, the same record as step 7), and then the delivery ends; their `CANCELLED` reaches the order through the delivery-status pipeline. If they refuse (their courier may already have the food), 409 `TRIP_NOT_CANCELLABLE` says why and nothing changes; another try waits a minute after the last one, and one while Borzo is being switched to Pidge waits for that.
- The reason goes to the audit trail. A released rider sees a fixed line, never the ops reason.

### Trip steps and statuses

`FleetStatusBridge` sends fleet statuses through the existing delivery-status pipeline as provider `craves_fleet`, while the trip's latest stage is the fleet's. `DELIVERY_STATUS_CHANGED` carries the optional `courierName`, `courierPhone`, `courierPhotoUrl` and `pickupEtaAt` (`contracts/events/delivery-status-changed-v1.schema.json`), filled for fleet riders and from Pidge's `fulfillment.rider`; order-service stores them in the columns the chef sees, and the customer sees the rider card. A fleet rider's `pickupEtaAt` is the accept time plus the travel-time model's minutes when the offer had them (`trip.pickup_minutes`), else plus the pickup distance at 20 km/h.

| Trip state | App step | Delivery status published | Reached by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SEARCHING` | (offer) | none | fleet stage open |
| `ASSIGNED` | `TO_KITCHEN` | `COURIER_ASSIGNED` + rider card | accept |
| `AT_PICKUP` | `AT_KITCHEN` | `AT_PICKUP` | `arrived-pickup` (and `food-not-ready`, no status) |
| `PICKED_UP` | `PICKED_UP` | `PICKED_UP`, then `IN_TRANSIT` once a real fix arrives after pickup | `picked-up` |
| `AT_DROP` | `AT_DROP` | `AT_DROPOFF` | `arrived-drop` |
| `DELIVERED` | `DELIVERED` | `DELIVERED` | `delivered` (OTP, or photo) |
| `FAILED` | `FAILED` | `FAILED` | `failed` after pickup |
| `CANCELLED` | `CANCELLED` | `CANCELLED`, unless another stage took the order | order cancelled, ops cancel, watchdog |

Each step is idempotent (a repeated key, or a step already taken, returns the trip; the key is bound to its first request, so another request with it is 422 `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`, see "Idempotency keys"), in order (else 409 `STEP_CONFLICT`; an ended trip is 409 `TRIP_NOT_ACTIVE`), and moves `trip.version`. The rider card is the first name and initial ("Ravi K.") and the rider's phone; no photo URL. `sealed` is false: orders have no sealed flag yet.

- **When a step happened** (app 1.1.0, blueprint A4). Every step may carry the tap: `ageMs` (how long before the request the rider tapped) and `fix` (the phone's fix then, with its own `ageMs`); the photo form carries them as fields. The step counts from `occurred` = now minus `ageMs` (at most 30 minutes), never before the trip's previous step (`accepted_at`, `arrived_kitchen_at`, `picked_up_at`, `arrived_drop_at`), and `arrived_kitchen_at`, `picked_up_at` and `arrived_drop_at` take that time. An earlier `arrived-pickup`, `food-not-ready` or `arrived-drop` raises waiting pay or shortens the door wait, so it counts only with the tap's own fix, taken within 2 minutes of the tap, real, accurate to 100 m, and within 300 m of the kitchen or 250 m of the drop; otherwise the step counts from now. Without `ageMs` (older apps) it is now, as before. The step's `trip_event` keeps `detail.ageMs` and the fix's `lat`, `lng` and `mock`.
- The free wait runs from the later of the arrival inside the kitchen zone (the tap's own fix when it was taken within 2 minutes of the tap, else a real fix under 2 minutes old within 300 m; checked at `arrived-pickup` and again at `food-not-ready`) and the chef's ready time, and stops at `picked-up`.
- `delivered {method: OTP, otp}` asks order-service once per attempt: 422 `OTP_INVALID` with `attemptsLeft` 4 to 1, then 423 `OTP_LOCKED` from the fifth wrong code on (the rider never sees "0 tries left"). An order accepted before delivery codes existed (order-service 404) has no code: 423 `OTP_LOCKED` at once, so the photo route opens. A repeated key with the same code gets its first answer without asking again; with another code it is 422 `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`.
- **A code typed offline** (app 1.1.0, blueprint A2, decision E4). The app keeps an unsent code encrypted on the phone (Keystore, at most 24 h, never in plain storage) and tells the rider "Delivery saved. You can go." It then sends `capturedOffline: true`. If that code is refused (`OTP_INVALID` or `OTP_LOCKED`), the rider has left the door, so ops gets `FLEET_OFFLINE_OTP_INVALID` (order, trip, rider) once per trip: a `trip_event` `OFFLINE_OTP_ALERTED` with the fixed key `offline-otp-alert` makes it once, and the alert goes on its own thread. The app keeps "Order code didn't match. Call support." on Home until the rider dismisses it. The trip waits at the drop, so the rider gets no new offers, until ops records the customer's answer on the console's Trips page ("Delivery codes to check", `GET /code-checks`, `POST /trips/{id}/code-check`): `DELIVERED` (they have the food: the trip is delivered with full pay, not held) or `NOT_DELIVERED` (they don't: the trip fails, `cancel_reason` says so, and the same pay is held with the reason while ops checks with the rider). Either reaches the order, and the rider is free again. A right code that arrives first ends the check too.
- `delivered` multipart `{method: PHOTO, callsMade, photo}` needs two calls (422 `CALLS_REQUIRED`) and the unreachable wait from the `arrived-drop` tap to the photo's tap (422 `WAIT_NOT_OVER`), or an OTP lock. Both taps are timed from their `ageMs` (app 1.1.0; up to 12 h), so a door with no signal or GPS still counts the wait when both steps sync later; the arrival's location evidence and 30-minute cap decide `arrived_drop_at`, not this. A tapped arrival never counts from before the accept, and the event keeps `doorWaitFrom` for ops' check of the held pay. Without ages it is `arrived_drop_at` to now. `failed CUSTOMER_UNREACHABLE` uses the same wait; a JPEG or PNG under 10 MB (422 `PHOTO_REQUIRED`). The photo goes to `riders/{riderId}/trips/{tripId}/`; the pay is held for ops. A retry for a trip already delivered gets the trip back, with its photo or without one (the photo is checked after that); the same key with another photo or another `callsMade` is 422 `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`.
- `failed {reason, note}` after pickup: `CUSTOMER_UNREACHABLE` (at the drop, after the wait) is full pay; `WRONG_ADDRESS`, `CUSTOMER_REFUSED`, `UNSAFE` are full pay plus the return. Held for ops.

### Pay and slots

Pay (`FleetPay`): base + drop km after the base km + pickup km after the free km (straight line × detour) + waiting after the free minutes, pro rata, in paise. Cancelled riding to the kitchen: pickup leg; at the kitchen: cancellation fee + pickup leg + waiting; after pickup: full + return. Every paid end stores the lines on the trip and publishes `FleetTripPayFinalized(tripId, riderId, rateCardVersion, lines, held, holdReason, finalizedAt)`; the money code writes the ledger.

Ops books a rider into a zone with a guaranteed hourly minimum inside the rate card's declared range; Home shows the next booked slot and its guarantee. Each minute a finished slot settles once: top-up = max(0, guarantee × in-zone hours − trip pay that finished in the slot) into `rider_slot_settlement`, on the slot's own in-zone seconds, so a shift across several slots pays each slot only for its own minutes. Declines never enter it. A break (Phase 4) adds no in-zone seconds to the slot: the guarantee pays for time a rider was available for offers.

## Money

### Earnings ledger

`FleetEarnings` (fleet flag) turns pay into money:

1. A trip that ends with pay publishes `FleetTripPayFinalized`. After the trip's transaction commits, the fleet pool credits it in its own transaction: a failed credit never undoes a delivery. The money job credits any finished trip still without `trip.pay_credited_at`, and every slot settlement without `credited_at`.
2. Each pay line becomes a `rider_earning` row with key `fleet-trip-pay/{tripId}/{component}/{revision}` (revision 1) and one balanced journal in the immutable ledger with the same `business_event_key`: debit `DELIVERY_EXPENSE`, credit the pooled `RIDER_PAYABLE`, lines tagged `delivery_attempt_id` = trip and `provider_id` = `craves_fleet`. The unique key (in both tables) makes a second credit impossible, and every trip's journal lines net to zero. A slot top-up is its own component, `slot-top-up`.
3. A row is pending until `withdrawable_at`, 24 h after the delivery. It counts towards the balance only once past that, with no active hold on its trip and no open support issue raised on the trip before then (an issue blocks that trip's pay until it is FIXED or 7 days pass).
4. A held trip (photo delivery, failed delivery, rider lost before pickup) gets a hold at credit with its own reason, released at 00:00 IST 7 days after the trip at the latest. Ops can place one hold per trip and release it early. Holds touch that trip only.
5. Adjustments are later revisions of a component (2 and up) with the reason the rider sees: payments admins make them on the console's Rider payouts page (`POST /trips/{id}/pay-adjustments`, `FleetEarnings.adjustTrip`), for a trip whose pay is credited. A positive one is pay like any other: pending 24 hours, with TDS withheld when TDS is on. A negative one is debt (debit `RIDER_PAYABLE`, credit `DELIVERY_EXPENSE`), recovered only from future pay at the weekly cap.
6. TDS (off by default): each credit withholds `rate × the rider's gross this financial year (from 1 April IST) − TDS already withheld this year`, nothing while the year's gross is at or under the threshold, at most the credit itself. It is its own row (`component` `tds`) and journal: debit `RIDER_PAYABLE`, credit `RIDER_TDS_PAYABLE`.

Postings for one ₹67 trip on the draft rate card (drop 4.2 km, pickup 1.8 km):

| Key | Debit | Credit | ₹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fleet-trip-pay/{trip}/base/1` | `DELIVERY_EXPENSE` | `RIDER_PAYABLE` | 40.00 |
| `fleet-trip-pay/{trip}/drop-km/1` | `DELIVERY_EXPENSE` | `RIDER_PAYABLE` | 19.80 |
| `fleet-trip-pay/{trip}/pickup-km/1` | `DELIVERY_EXPENSE` | `RIDER_PAYABLE` | 7.20 |
| `fleet-trip-pay/{trip}/tds/1` (TDS on, threshold crossed, 1%) | `RIDER_PAYABLE` | `RIDER_TDS_PAYABLE` | 0.67 |

A photo delivery posts the same journals; only the hold differs.

### Payouts

`FleetPayouts` (fleet flag). Nothing moves money by itself; a person uploads every file to the bank.

- **Balance.** available = released pay (net of TDS) − payouts that did not fail (requested withdrawals included) − debt recovered. Withdrawable = available − this week's recovery (outstanding debt, at most the weekly cap), never below zero.
- **Weekly payout.** A WEEKLY file on a Monday pays every rider with a verified, unpaused account their whole withdrawable balance, by IMPS, no minimum. Making it records this week's recovery (`rider_recovery`). Riders with nothing, a negative balance or no account are left out. Debt is never debited from a bank.
- **Daily withdrawal.** One a day per rider (IST), free, up to the withdrawable balance. It is a payout (`DAILY_WITHDRAWAL`, date = tomorrow) at once, so the balance drops at once; the DAILY_WITHDRAWAL file for a date takes every request for that date or before not yet in a file.
- **Never twice.** A payout belongs to one file. A sent payout stays processing until ops marks its UTR or a final failure; nothing re-sends it.
- **Failure.** FAILED with the bank's reason: the money stays in the balance, and the account is paused (`features` false, `PAYOUTS_PAUSED`, left out of files) until ops sets a new account in person or confirms one the rider sends.
- **Cancel before upload.** A file never sent (CREATED or APPROVED) can be cancelled with a reason once the admin confirms it was not uploaded. A weekly file's payouts and this week's recoveries are deleted, so the next weekly file pays and recovers the same money; a daily file's withdrawals leave the file and wait for the next one. Its date can then be made again.
- **Bank reversal.** A payout the bank returns after its UTR is recorded as a reversal: FAILED with "Returned by the bank: …", its UTR kept for the statement, the money back in the balance and the account paused, as for a failure.
- **Ledger.** Only a UTR posts: `fleet-payout/{payoutId}/paid`, debit `RIDER_PAYABLE`, credit `BANK`, evidence `utr/{UTR}`. A reversal posts `fleet-payout/{payoutId}/reversed` with `reversal_of` the paid journal and its lines inverted, evidence `utr/{UTR}/reversed`. So `RIDER_PAYABLE` = all rider pay − payouts that reached a bank and stayed there.

### Payout runbook for ops

Weekly: on Monday, approved by 13:00, sent by 15:00 IST. Daily withdrawals: every morning, paid by 13:00.

1. **Create the file.** A payments admin (the maker) makes it in the console: Payouts → WEEKLY with Monday's date, or DAILY_WITHDRAWAL with today's date. Never type amounts or build a file in a spreadsheet.
2. **Download** the bank file (audited). Check the total and the count against the console.
3. **Second approver.** Another payments admin (the checker) checks the file total against the console, the count against riders with a balance, any payout above twice the rider's usual week, any payout account changed in the last 7 days, and any payout that already has a UTR; then approves. The maker cannot approve (403 `SAME_APPROVER`).
4. **Bank upload.** Upload the approved file once to the bank's IMPS bulk upload (use the bank's own two-user approval if it has one), then **mark sent**. Never upload a file twice, and never re-send a payout the bank still shows as processing (up to T+3 working days).
5. **Mark UTRs.** Enter each confirmed payout's UTR within 2 hours; the rider sees it. For a final failure (account closed, invalid), mark it failed with the bank's reason: the money stays in the rider's balance and their payouts pause; set a new account with the rider in person, and the next file includes them. If the bank returns a payout after its UTR, record the bank reversal on that row with the bank's reason: the same pause follows.
   - A mistake found before upload (a wrong date, an account that changed): **cancel the file** with the reason and the not-uploaded check, fix the cause, and make it again. Never cancel a file that might have reached the bank; once it is sent, mark each payout instead.
6. **Reconcile** daily: every UTR on the bank statement matches a payout with the same amount; the file total equals its PAID payouts plus those still pending; `RIDER_PAYABLE` debits for `RIDER_PAYOUT_PAID` equal the PAID payouts; no file stays SENT with pending payouts past T+3 working days without a call to the bank.

### Support and SOS

- A rider reports a problem on a trip, a payout or nothing (`MISSING_PAY`, `WRONG_DISTANCE`, `PAYOUT_NOT_RECEIVED`, `WRONG_DEDUCTION`, `APP_PROBLEM`, `OTHER`). Support acknowledges within 24 hours and fixes within 72, on the console's Rider support page: Acknowledge, then Mark fixed with what was done, both audited with who did it. Support tells the rider by phone or WhatsApp; the app does not show the resolution. Fixing releases pay the issue held; an open issue stops holding the trip's pay after 7 days anyway. To correct the pay itself, adjust the trip ("Earnings ledger" step 5).
- SOS: `FleetSupportService.sos` stores the press (`sos_event`) first, then calls `/internal/v1/ops-alerts` (kind `SOS`): "SOS from rider <name>", the full phone, `https://maps.google.com/?q=<lat>,<lng>` with the fix time, and the trip's order code. Set `CRAVES_OPS_ALERT_EMAILS` to the ops lead. `alerted_at` is set when notification-service accepted it; otherwise the alert is a `CRAVES_ALERT alert=SOS` log line with the same data, and the console's Rider support page marks the press "Email not sent".

## Test mode for one rider and one kitchen

1. Deploy in the order above. The runtime tool (`scripts/riders/rider_pilot_runtime.py`, run from Azure Cloud Shell; stages status, wire, enable and disable; step by step in `GO-LIVE.md` §4) sets what follows. Keep `CRAVES_FLEET_TEST_MODE=true` (the default) and set `CRAVES_FLEET_ENABLED=true`, `CRAVES_FLEET_RIDER_API_ENABLED=true` and `CRAVES_FLEET_LOCATION_INGEST_ENABLED=true`, plus the storage, bank-data, support-phone and internal URL settings (`CRAVES_AUTH_INTERNAL_BASE_URL`, `CRAVES_USER_CHEF_INTERNAL_BASE_URL`, `CRAVES_FLEET_ORDER_BASE_URL`, `CRAVES_NOTIFICATION_INTERNAL_BASE_URL`, `CRAVES_INTERNAL_SERVICE_KEY`). In notification-service set `CRAVES_REALTIME_PUSH_ENABLED=true` and `CRAVES_OPS_ALERT_EMAILS`.
2. The rider installs the app, signs in with their phone and applies (or ops adds them in the console with `POST /riders` and they sign in once so `/me` links them). They send their documents and payout account from the app. Ops opens them from Riders → To review, marks each document (with its expiry date) and the payout account against the bank proof, and approves in one press (`FLOWS.md` §5), which also adds the rider to the pilot riders while test mode is on.
3. Add the kitchen to the pilot in the console (`PUT /pilot/kitchens/{chefId}`, the chef's identity id); a rider approved before the one-step decision is added with `PUT /pilot/riders/{riderId}`. Dispatch asks `FleetTestMode.riderAllowed` and `kitchenAllowed` before it offers a trip, so nobody else is touched. The kitchen is known only for orders checked out with the finance quote (`finance_issued_snapshot`).
4. Publish the rate card in the console (the first one may start at once), then set dispatch: `shadow` first (it only logs `FLEET_SHADOW`), and `live` only after order-service with the `craves_fleet` hand-over is deployed. `off` stops dispatch at once. `GET /setup` shows what is in force.
5. The rider registers the phone, goes online with a selfie, and the allow-listed kitchen accepts an order: the offer rings within the fleet stage. For money, turn on `CRAVES_LEDGER_POSTING_ENABLED` (the existing journal gate); credits wait until then.
